Where It Went – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026 · App package: com.trunglabs.timeinplace · Developer: TrungLabs

Where It Went is an Android app that automatically records the places you stay at and for how long, and summarises your days and weeks. This policy explains what data the app handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. In short: your location history and journal stay on your phone; the only copy that can leave it is the backup you choose to keep in your own Google Drive. The maps in the app are drawn with Google Maps, which requests map tiles from Google (see section 2.2); place names are looked up online from the coordinates of your stays (section 2.1); and a few features send limited data as described below.

Summary

  • We (the developer) run no server that receives your location history, photos, notes or expenses.
  • There are no ads and no sale or sharing of your data with advertisers or data brokers.
  • The app sends anonymous crash reports (Google Firebase Crashlytics), and, only if you switch it on in Settings, anonymous usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase). Neither contains your location, places, money or notes. See section 2.5.
  • Signing in to Google for backup, reading payment notifications, AI analysis with Gemini, receipt scanning, photo suggestions, QR sharing and "Pause when still" (physical activity) are optional; the app works without them.

2. Data that leaves your phone

2.1 Place-name lookup

To suggest the name of a place where you just stayed, its coordinates (and nothing else) are sent to a map data provider: Huawei Petal Maps (HUAWEI Site Kit), or, if that service is unavailable or its monthly allowance for your device is used up, OpenStreetMap services (Overpass and Nominatim). When a new stay appears in the Journal, the app first asks for the one place at that spot (reverse geocoding) and asks you whether that is where you are; if you say no, it asks for the places nearby. Each spot is looked up at most once a month: results are cached on your phone for a month. When using OpenStreetMap services while you are signed in with Google, your Google email address is added to the request's User-Agent as a contact address, as that service's usage policy asks. There is no switch for this in the app (in trial mode nothing is looked up); to stop it, stop recording or uninstall the app.

See Huawei's privacy policy and the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy.

2.2 Maps display (Google Maps)

Maps in the app (the places map, confirming a visit, adding or editing a place, privacy rules by place) are always shown with the Google Maps SDK, whether or not "Look up place names online" is on. To draw a map, the SDK requests map tiles from Google for the area you are viewing, so Google receives technical information such as your IP address, device information and the map area requested, as described in the Google Privacy Policy. The app does not send your visit history, place names or notes to Google Maps.

2.3 Backup to Google Drive (optional)

Signing in with Google is optional and only needed for backup. If you sign in and turn backup on, the app stores a backup file (and copies of photos you attached to visits and movements) in the hidden app-data folder of your own Google Drive. The app requests only the drive.appdata permission, so it cannot see any other file in your Drive. The backup includes: places, visits, notes, expenses, goals, budgets, recurring transactions, debts, your people and their photos (small copies), groups of people, who you were with, privacy rules, trips, movement kinds (walking, cycling, in a vehicle) and their times, most settings, the list of removed photos, cached place lookups, and photos attached to visits and movements. It does not include raw location points, your account details, the online-lookup choice or pause state. Backups can be sent only over Wi-Fi if you choose. Each phone keeps its own backup file there too, with the phone's model name, so that you can merge the data of another of your phones into this one ("Merge into this phone"); merging happens on your phone and deletes nothing. We, the developer, cannot access this backup.

You can remove the backup at any time from Settings → Google Drive backup → "Delete backup on Drive".

2.4 Sharing a period by QR code (optional)

You can create a GIF of QR codes that contains a day, week or month (at most one month) of your journal and give it to someone else. You choose what it contains: places, times and categories, and optionally coordinates, notes and expenses. The content is compressed and encrypted on your phone with a key derived from a PIN that you set; the first QR code only states how many codes there are and their random identifiers. Whoever receives the GIF needs the PIN to read it. The PIN is shown to you so that you can tell the recipient; it is not sent anywhere. The GIF leaves your phone only when you share it yourself (for example in a chat app); we do not upload it and we do not receive it. Anyone who has both the GIF and the PIN can read the content, so share the PIN only with the person you mean to share with.

When you receive a share, it is stored on your phone only, encrypted with a key held by the Android Keystore. It is shown as read-only, is not added to your own journal or summaries, is not included in backups, and you can delete it at any time.

2.5 Crash reports and anonymous usage statistics (Google Firebase)

The app uses two services provided by Google. Firebase Crashlytics sends a crash report when the app crashes or hits an unexpected error; this is on and there is no switch for it in the app. Google Analytics for Firebase counts which features are used, anonymously; this is also on, without a switch. Neither is active in trial mode.

Usage statistics. Each event has a fixed name and a few coarse values, for example "a visit was confirmed (automatically or by you)", "a goal was created (at least or at most, per day, week or month)", "the summary was opened (week)", "a share was created (covering 2 to 7 days)", the name of a screen (such as "Settings"), the interface language, the theme, the recording mode and accuracy you chose, and whether a permission was granted or denied. Counts and durations are grouped into ranges.

Crash reports. When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, a report is sent with the technical error and stack trace, the app version, Android version and device model, whether the app was in the foreground, the app's internal mode, the chosen accuracy mode, the database version and the interface language.

What is never included: your location or coordinates, place names or addresses, visit history, amounts of money, notification text, photos or file names, the PIN of a shared QR code, your e-mail or Google account, or anything you typed. We do not set a user identifier, we turn off the advertising identifier and ad personalization, and the data is not used for advertising or sold.

Firebase creates a randomly generated app-instance identifier to count devices. Google processes the data as our service provider; see the Google Privacy Policy and Firebase privacy information. Event data is kept for up to 14 months and crash reports for up to 90 days. Switching usage statistics off stops new events; to reset the identifier, clear the app's storage or reinstall.

2.6 AI analysis with Google Gemini (Where It Went Pro, optional)

AI analysis is part of the paid Where It Went Pro plan (monthly, yearly or a one-time lifetime purchase; the former Ultra subscription counts as Pro). Without Pro, notifications and receipts are read with the app's basic reading and nothing is sent for this purpose. With a subscription, "AI analysis" is on by default (you can turn it off in Settings), and data is sent to Google Gemini through Firebase AI Logic as follows:

  • Notifications: the text of notifications from the apps you chose for "Spending from notifications" is sent with account and card numbers, balances, one-time codes, transaction references, phone numbers, email addresses and links masked on your phone first (the amount, the merchant and names stay), to find the amount, the currency, whether money was spent or received, the category and a short summary, and to tell whether it is a transaction at all (advertisements, loan offers, codes and reminders are then not recorded).
  • Receipts: the photo of a receipt you scan is sent made smaller (about 1024 pixels on the long side) to read the total, the currency, the category, the shop and the time printed.
  • Sorting transactions: when you tap "Sort" on the Spending screen, the confirmed transactions of the period on screen that have a note but no category: the note and, for a transaction read from a notification, the package name of the app it came from (up to 30 at a time). No amounts, place names or coordinates. Each transaction is sent once, even if you tap again for a longer period while it is being sorted. While AI analysis is on, the app also does this by itself in the background for the last year of transactions, at most once a day and only while the phone is charging, on Wi-Fi and with enough battery (at most 90 transactions each time); the same data is sent as when you tap.
  • Place suggestions: when you confirm a stay, the names and types of the nearby places the app found, the time of day, the weekday and the length of the stay; no coordinates.
  • Goal suggestions: hours per week by type of place for the last four weeks and the goals you already have; no place names, notes or coordinates.
  • Ask AI: the question you type, the last few questions and answers of the same chat (so a follow-up is understood), a fixed description of the app's features, and totals worked out on your phone for this and last week and this and last month: hours by type of place, time away from home and on the move, the number of places and of new places, average times you left and came back home, spending and money received by transaction category in each currency, your budgets and their progress, your time goals, recurring payments due in the next two weeks (category, amount, date) and open debt totals per currency. Never sent: coordinates, addresses, place names, notes, people's names or notification text. The AI uses this to answer, compare and suggest; a few questions about other periods are still answered on your phone. When you ask the app to record something (a transaction, a budget, a time goal), the AI only proposes it: nothing is saved until you tap Confirm. Do not type personal details you do not want sent. Your chats are kept only on your phone (not in the Drive backup) until you delete them in the chat history.
  • Weekly recap: if you turned on the weekly summary notification, its text is written by the AI from this and last week's totals (the same kinds of totals as above, never names, places or coordinates).

Google processes this data as our service provider under the paid Gemini API terms, which do not allow Google to use it to improve its products. The app keeps only the results (amounts, categories, short summaries and suggestions), never the notification text or the photo; the questions and answers of Ask AI stay in your chat history on the phone; Requests go over an encrypted connection and are protected by Firebase App Check. Turn the switch off, or let the subscription end, and nothing more is sent. The setting is not part of your Google Drive backup. See the Google Privacy Policy and the Gemini API terms.

2.7 Purchases

Where It Went Pro (a monthly or yearly subscription, or a one-time lifetime purchase) is bought, switched, renewed and cancelled through Google Play, which handles your payment details; the developer never sees them. The app only asks Google Play which purchase this Google account has, and keeps that answer on your phone. Free trials are granted by Google Play, one per Google account.

2.8 Assistants on your phone (App Functions, Android 16 and later)

On Android 16 and later, the app offers a few actions to assistants on your phone that the system allows to use them (for example Google Gemini, with the system's App Functions permission): add a transaction, total your spending for today, this week, month or year, sum up your time (places visited, hours by type of place, the place you are at now), and pause or resume recording. These actions run in the app on your phone when the assistant asks; what the assistant does with the answer is covered by the assistant's own privacy policy. The app sends nothing anywhere for this purpose itself. You can stop it by not using such an assistant or by revoking its permissions in Android settings.

2.9 Import and export (optional)

Import from Google Timeline: when you choose a Timeline file exported from Google Maps (Timeline.json from your phone, or the "Semantic Location History" of Google Takeout, as .json or .zip), the app reads it on your phone and adds the stays it contains as visits (time, coordinates of the stay, and the place name or Home / Work label when the file has one). Raw location points in the file are not imported. The file is not sent anywhere and is not kept after reading; the imported visits are stored like any other visit and are part of your Google Drive backup if you turn backup on.

Export data: the app can write your visits (date, times, place, category, status, latitude and longitude) and transactions (date, time, amount, currency, direction, category, note, place) as CSV files into a folder you choose with the system file picker. The app does not send these files anywhere; what happens to them afterwards is up to you.

3. What we do not do

  • We do not sell your data, share it for advertising, or use it for profiling.
  • We do not upload your location history, photos, notes or expenses to any server operated by the developer, and we do not put them in usage statistics or crash reports.
  • We do not read notifications of apps you have not chosen, and we do not store the content of notifications or full account numbers.
  • Trial mode uses sample data made on your phone. It records nothing about you, sends nothing anywhere and can be exited at any time, which deletes the sample data.

4. Your choices and rights

  • Location: change or revoke location and background location in Android Settings → Apps → Where It Went → Permissions. The app then records only while open, or not at all.
  • Physical activity: optional; turn "Pause when still" off in Settings, or revoke "Physical activity" in Android Settings → Apps → Where It Went → Permissions. The app then takes your location at the rate of the accuracy mode you chose.
  • Pause or restrict recording: use the quick pause or time/place privacy rules in Settings; no location is stored during those periods.
  • Photos and notifications: both are opt-in and can be turned off at any time in Settings; notification access can also be revoked in Android Settings.
  • Backup: optional; signing out stops backup and keeps data on your phone.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports: anonymous and always on; to stop them you can uninstall the app or contact us.
  • Access and deletion: all your data is inside the app and can be deleted there (delete a visit's note, expenses or photos, delete a place). Uninstalling the app or clearing its storage in Android Settings removes everything stored on the phone.

5. Deleting your data

Because the developer holds no copy of your data, deleting it is done on your side:

  1. On your phone: uninstall Where It Went, or Android Settings → Apps → Where It Went → Storage → Clear storage.
  2. In Google Drive (only if you used backup): open Settings → Google Drive backup → "Delete backup on Drive" in the app before uninstalling. You can also remove the app's hidden data at Google Drive → Settings → Manage apps and revoke its access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

6. Data retention

Data on your phone is kept until you delete it or remove the app. Cached place lookups expire after a limited time. Dismissed notification-based expenses are removed automatically after about 30 days. Google Firebase keeps crash reports for up to 90 days and, if you turned them on, usage statistics for up to 14 months. A Google Drive backup stays in your Drive until you delete it.

7. Children

Where It Went is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect their data.

8. Security

Data is stored in the app's private storage on your device and is protected by Android's app sandbox. Network requests use HTTPS. No system is perfectly secure, so keep your device locked and updated.

9. Changes to this policy

If the app's data practices change, this page will be updated and the date at the top changed. Material changes will also be reflected in the app's permission and settings screens.
